SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

How Daywise
learns.

Daywise trains a personalized reward model from your study sessions and ratings. Weighted LinUCB uses learned productivity and mood estimates to rank study options. A separate language model coordinates actions, while calendar tools enforce scheduling constraints.

PERSONALIZED MODEL UPDATES

Your feedback changes the next prediction.

Each completed, rated session adds a training example: study context is the input, and your productivity and mood ratings are the targets. The model updates its reward estimates as new feedback becomes available, so repeated patterns in your places, study setups and session lengths can influence later recommendations.

  1. 01 Record session context
  2. 02 Rate productivity + mood
  3. 03 Update reward model
  4. 04 Rank future options

What is trained: the personal linear reward model behind LinUCB. The underlying language model is not fine-tuned. Distraction attribution changes how strongly an observation contributes.

Design choices and benefits

01 / SEPARATE RESPONSIBILITIES

Language interpretation is separate from scheduling validation.

The coordinator selects from a validated action schema. Calendar availability and conflict resolution are computed by code, so an LLM cannot invent a valid opening or bypass approval.

02 / ONLINE PERSONALIZATION

Your ratings train personalized reward estimates.

Productivity and mood are separate fitted reward targets. New linked session feedback updates the model used for later predictions. Recurring patterns in your recorded study contexts can shape rankings; uncertainty keeps sparse data from looking conclusive.

03 / CONTROLLED PROACTIVITY

Meaningful changes trigger a review.

Persistent state tracks location, availability, session progress, and previous decisions. Quiet hours, cooldowns, active sessions, and a model-call budget gate proactive messages.

04 / AUDITABLE EXECUTION

Every calendar edit has a reviewable proposal.

The review contains old/new times and a concrete reason. Approval checks the event version and destination again. Repeated approvals recover the same edit instead of creating a duplicate.

How optional session notes inform decisions

After a study session, you can describe anything else that affected it. The language model extracts sentiment, social context and explicitly mentioned factors, then asks you to confirm them. Confirmed notes accompany your actual ratings when the coordinator considers future prompts. A difficult assignment can explain a low rating without proving that the library was a poor place to study.

These notes provide context for reasoning. They do not replace your ratings or directly change LinUCB scores. Repeated confirmed factors can provide descriptive evidence, but they do not establish causes or describe your current situation.

Current model scope

Daywise uses a contextual-bandit approach, not an end-to-end reinforcement-learning policy. Features describe place, study setup, workspace conditions, duration, and missing data. Time of day and subject are not predictive features in this MVP. Distraction is collected after the session and affects feedback weighting; it is not a third predicted reward.

Environment scores are associations, not evidence that a place caused an outcome. Calendar-conflict proposals use a deterministic minimum-movement rule, not a learned claim that another hour will improve productivity.
